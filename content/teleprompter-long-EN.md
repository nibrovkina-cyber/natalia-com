---

A year ago I was doing the same thing every single week.

New client. Dental clinic. Or a salon. Or a law firm.

I'd sit down, open ChatGPT, write a prompt from scratch. Then again. Then again — slightly different this time, because the last version didn't quite land.

Every single time. The same cycle.

I thought that was just what marketing work looks like. You work with your hands, you think with your hands, and that's the output.

And then I realized: the problem wasn't the AI. The problem was that I had no system. Every single time I was explaining to the AI who I am, what I do, and what rules I follow.

Today I'm going to show you how that changed.

I'm going to show you Claude Skills. These aren't prompts. They're not chatbots. They're your expertise, packaged into a file that works with any client.

Let's go.

---

This is how every new client starts for me now.

I open Claude Code Desktop. Free application from Anthropic — link in the description.

Here I have a folder: demo-recording / medea-dent.

Medea Dent is a real dental clinic in Moscow. Four doctors. Experience ranging from 10 to 25 years. Free consultation.

The folder has three sections.

First — _foundations. This is where Schwartz, Ogilvy, Hopkins live. Three people who figured out how persuasive writing works. I didn't invent these rules — they worked 50 years ago and they work now.

Second — context. There's brand-memory.json here. Client data: address, ratings, pricing, audience objections, what to say and what to never say.

Third — SOPs. Standard operating procedures. Concrete steps for each task.

All of this is what Claude reads before it writes anything.

---

Now watch what happens when I run a skill.

Skill number one — research.

I ask Claude to run a competitor analysis for Medea Dent. It opens the SOP, pulls the rules from Schwartz, the data from brand-memory — and outputs a strategic brief.

Top 3 competitors. Our competitive window. Three audience triggers.

In fifteen minutes. Without me explaining what Schwartz Level 2 is every single time.

---

Skill number two — hero copy.

This is the first screen of the landing page. The most important piece of real estate. According to Ogilvy, the headline gets read five times more than the body copy.

Claude takes the research output and writes three hero variants. Schwartz Level 2 — that's the person who knows they have a problem but doesn't know the solution.

Variant A — name the fear directly. Scared of pain at the dentist?

Variant B — price transparency. Your price is locked in before treatment starts.

Variant C — social proof. 4.4 on Google Maps. 12 reviews.

Each variant comes with an explanation — why I made this choice, which rule it's based on.

That matters. Not just copy. Copy with reasoning I can defend to a client.

---

Skill number three — audience analysis.

Who exactly comes to this clinic. What's their trigger. What are they afraid to ask. What do they need to hear before they'll book.

Three segments: neighborhood residents, office workers from nearby buildings, parents with children.

---

Skill number four — content calendar.

Twelve posts for the month. Each post tied to a specific objection from brand-memory. Format, hook, call to action.

---

Skill number five — email sequence.

Five emails after the first visit. Retention, return, referral.

---

All of this — one client, one folder, one system.

---

Now for the honest part.

This isn't magic. Claude isn't thinking for you.

If you just download the skills and ask Claude to write copy — you'll get copy. Good copy. Maybe very good copy.

But without a system behind the tool — you're just automating chaos.

The skill works because the methodology is baked in. Because Claude knows that Schwartz Level 2 isn't just words, it's a specific approach to a specific audience. Because brand-memory prevents Claude from writing "high-quality services" when that phrase is on the banned list.

A system is when AI carries your rules — not when it guesses what you want.
